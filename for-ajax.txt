THE TWENTY-FOURTH — what your telescope would have seen
For Ajax, who can't hold a phone. Pick a year — any way you like — and read her what was up.
Every sky is worked out for nine at night on 24 October, over Eastern Canada.
Two of the years have something else in them. You'll know when you find one.

== 24 October 1926 ==
The brightest thing up after the moon, in the south: Jupiter, named for the king of the gods, and king of this sky. Somewhere low in the south-east was Mira — “the wonderful” — a star that fades out of sight and comes back over eleven months. Whether it was showing that night depended on its year. Cassiopeia sat high in the north-east — a W of stars; the vain queen in her chair.
